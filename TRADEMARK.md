# Trademark and project name

The Apache License, Version 2.0 grants copyright and patent rights. **It does not
grant trademark rights.** Section 6 states:

> This License does not grant permission to use the trade names, trademarks,
> service marks, or product names of the Licensor, except as required for
> reasonable and customary use in describing the origin of the Work and
> reproducing the content of the NOTICE file.

So you may say your project is "built on AI Toolkit" or "uses code from AI
Toolkit" — that is descriptive, customary use. You may not present a fork as
though it were the original.

## What needs no permission

- Using the code for any purpose, commercial or not, under Apache-2.0.
- Stating factually that your work derives from AI Toolkit, and linking back to
  the original repository.
- Reproducing [`NOTICE`](NOTICE) verbatim, which the license requires anyway.

## What needs permission

- Naming a fork or derivative "AI Toolkit" or a confusingly similar name.
- Using the name or logo to imply endorsement, sponsorship, or official status
  for your fork, product, or service.
- Distributing a modified version under the original name without marking it as
  modified.

## Forks

If you publish a fork or a derivative distribution, rename it. Pick a name that
is clearly distinguishable from "AI Toolkit", and state in your README that your
version is unaffiliated with, and not endorsed by, the original project. Keep
`LICENSE` and `NOTICE` intact, and mark files you changed as changed — both are
Apache-2.0 Section 4 requirements regardless of naming.
